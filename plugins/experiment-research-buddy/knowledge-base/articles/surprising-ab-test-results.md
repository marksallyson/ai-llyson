# Surprising A/B Test Results

## At a Glance
Ron Kohavi et al. (Microsoft, via exp-platform.com). A collection of real A/B test results from Microsoft that contradicted expert intuition — the empirical case for always testing rather than relying on expert judgment.

## Why They Matter
This resource is the single most effective argument for experimentation culture. When stakeholders say "we know this will work" or "we don't need to test this," the answer is: experienced engineers and PMs at Microsoft were wrong about A/B test outcomes in roughly a third of cases. The same pattern holds everywhere it's been measured.

## Key Contributions
- Documented multiple examples where expert intuition predicted a positive outcome and the experiment showed neutral or negative effects (and vice versa)
- Includes the famous "Bing revenue experiment" where a seemingly minor ad ranking change generated $100M+ in annual revenue — no expert had predicted the magnitude
- Establishes the empirical base rate: ~1/3 of tested ideas that experts believe will work actually show neutral or negative results
- Used by Kohavi in virtually every talk about why organizations need experimentation culture

## Takeaways for Practice
1. **Use this collection when stakeholders push to skip a test.** "We already know this works" is the most common path to shipping features that quietly hurt users.
2. **The base rate matters for prioritization.** If only ~1/3 of "sure things" pan out, you need to be running more tests, not fewer.
3. **Applies directly at Ibotta.** Brand teams and PMs often have strong opinions about which offer formats, values, or placements will drive redemption. The empirical record says test it.
4. **The most surprising results are the most valuable.** A test that finds the expected effect teaches you nothing new. A test that contradicts expert intuition is the highest-ROI experiment you ran.
