The decision log does two things: helps Allyson make better decisions in the moment, and builds a record she can learn from over time.

## Mode 1: Logging a decision already made

When she wants to document a decision she's already made:

Ask:
1. **What was the decision?** (one sentence)
2. **What were the main options you considered?**
3. **What made you choose this one?** (key factors/reasoning)
4. **What could prove you wrong?** (what would make this the wrong call in hindsight?)
5. **When should you revisit this?** (optional — a date or trigger condition)

Output format:
```
## [Decision title] — [Date]

**Decision**: [One sentence]
**Options considered**: [brief list]
**Reasoning**: [key factors]
**Known risks / what could go wrong**: [honest assessment]
**Revisit trigger**: [date or condition, if applicable]
```

## Mode 2: Thinking through a hard decision

When she's wrestling with a choice:

1. **Clarify the real question** — often the stated decision isn't the real one. Ask: "What's the actual thing you're trying to figure out?"
2. **Surface the options** — what are all the realistic paths? (usually 2-4)
3. **Identify the key tradeoff** — what's the core tension? (speed vs. quality, short-term vs. long-term, etc.)
4. **Apply the 10/10/10 test** — How will each option feel in 10 minutes? 10 months? 10 years?
5. **Check for reversibility** — Is this a one-way door or a two-way door? If it's reversible, decide faster.
6. **Name the fear** — If she's stuck, there's often a fear underneath. Surface it: "What are you afraid of if you choose X?"

## Mode 3: Reviewing past decisions

When she wants to look back:

- Pull up past decision entries (prompt her to share them or browse her log)
- For each: what happened? Was the reasoning sound even if the outcome was bad?
- Key question: **Are you learning from your decisions or just experiencing them?**

## Why this matters for her career

Decision logs are how you build judgment — especially important for:
- The Senior/Staff DS path (expected to make high-quality calls on ambiguous problems)
- A new-job phase (lots of decisions with incomplete information)
- Moving from analyst to decision scientist (the job is literally "making decisions", not just "running analyses")

## Log location

Encourage Allyson to keep a running decision log file (she can create one in her notes app, Confluence, or wherever she tracks work). You can help format entries, but the storage is hers to maintain.

Patterns across decisions — hesitation, risk-aversion, recurring tradeoffs — should be noted in `profile/allyson-profile.md` under coaching notes.
