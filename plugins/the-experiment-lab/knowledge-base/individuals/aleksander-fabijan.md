# Aleksander Fabijan

## At a Glance
Aleksander Fabijan holds a PhD from Malmö University and is a researcher on the Microsoft ExP team. He created the Experimentation Growth Model — the Crawl/Walk/Run/Fly maturity framework — which is the best published framework for assessing and scaling an organization's experimentation capabilities.

## Why They Matter
Fabijan's unique contribution is on the organizational side of experimentation: not statistical methods, but how you scale from "a few engineers running experiments" to "every team makes decisions from experiments." His Crawl/Walk/Run/Fly model is used by companies from startups to Fortune 500s to diagnose where they are and what to invest in next. If you want to make the case for expanding Ibotta's experimentation capabilities, this framework gives you the vocabulary and the roadmap.

## Key Contributions
- **Experimentation Growth Model (Crawl/Walk/Run/Fly)**: four-stage model for organizational experimentation maturity; each stage has specific characteristics, capabilities, and prerequisites
  - *Crawl*: experiments are ad-hoc, no shared infrastructure, no shared metrics
  - *Walk*: shared platform, consistent metric definitions, but limited to technical teams
  - *Run*: non-technical teams can launch experiments; experiment reviews are formalized
  - *Fly*: continuous experimentation is the default for every product change; experiments drive roadmap
- **"The Evolution of Continuous Experimentation in Software Product Development"** (Fabijan et al., ICSE 2017): the academic paper documenting the growth model
- **"Diagnosing Online Controlled Experimentation"** (Fabijan et al., 2018): how to identify and fix bottlenecks in an experimentation program
- **Human and organizational factors in experimentation**: published on why organizations fail to adopt experimentation even when the tools are available — culture, incentives, and trust gaps

## Takeaways for Practice
- **Use the Crawl/Walk/Run/Fly model to diagnose Ibotta's current state** before making any infrastructure investment. If you're at "Walk" (shared platform, technical teams only), the next investment should be non-technical access — not more statistical sophistication.
- **Read the ICSE 2017 paper as a template for an internal audit.** Go through each stage's criteria and score Ibotta honestly. This gives you an objective basis for a maturity roadmap pitch to leadership.
- **Fabijan's work on organizational failure modes is more useful than success stories** for identifying what might go wrong. Read his papers on why well-resourced teams still fail to build experimentation cultures.
- **The "Fly" stage — where experiments drive roadmap — requires leadership to accept that some ideas will be killed by data.** Fabijan's papers are honest about the cultural resistance this creates. Document these failure modes when building internal buy-in.
- His personal site has slides and papers freely available.
