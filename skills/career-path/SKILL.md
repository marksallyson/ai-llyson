## How to run a career check-in

When she asks about her career path, work through these questions (not all at once — pick what's most relevant):

1. **Where are you right now?** — What are you working on, what's going well, what feels hard?
2. **Where do you want to be?** — How clear is her picture of "Senior DS" or "Staff DS"? Help define it concretely if fuzzy.
3. **What's the gap?** — Between her current state and her target, what's actually missing? Skills, visibility, relationships, track record?
4. **What's the next milestone?** — Not the destination — just the next concrete thing that moves her forward.
5. **What's in the way?** — Blockers, fears, constraints?

## Senior / Staff DS progression framework

Use this as a reference when helping her understand the path:

### Analyst → Decision Scientist (she's making this transition now)
- Shift from "what happened" → "why it happened" and "what should we do"
- Begins owning analytical questions, not just executing them
- Starting to run A/B tests and use causal methods

### Decision Scientist → Senior Decision Scientist
- Consistently delivers work that changes decisions or strategy
- Owns a product area or analytical domain
- Mentors others, raises the team's bar
- Trusted to scope and structure ambiguous problems independently

### Senior → Staff Decision Scientist
- Organizational impact beyond a single product area
- Sets methodology standards for the team
- Works on the hardest, most ambiguous problems
- Often has a "specialty" — experimentation, causal inference, ML, etc.

## Skills to build toward Senior/Staff

Surface these as relevant to her situation:

**Technical**
- Experimentation / A/B testing design and analysis
- Causal inference methods (diff-in-diff, regression discontinuity, etc.)
- Predictive modeling (not just descriptive analytics)
- SQL → Python/R for statistical work

**Influence & craft**
- Structuring ambiguous problems into clear analytical frameworks
- Communicating statistical findings to non-technical stakeholders
- Writing strong analytical memos / decision documents
- Saying "no" to low-leverage work

**Relationships & visibility**
- Building trust with product and engineering partners
- Getting credit for impact (linking your work to outcomes)
- Mentoring / helping others on the team
